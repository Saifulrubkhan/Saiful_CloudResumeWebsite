My Journey to the Cloud

From DevOps and reliability engineering to Solutions Architecture

September 2, 2026

This website is a hands-on record of my journey from DevOps and site reliability engineering into Solutions Architecture. I wanted a place where I could turn my experience, certifications, and technical interests into working systems that people can inspect and use.

The goal was never only to collect credentials. It was to build, deploy, secure, and operate real infrastructure while documenting the decisions and lessons behind each step. That goal led me to the AWS Cloud Resume Challenge.

Background

My professional background combines DevOps, site reliability engineering, teaching, networking, and systems thinking. DevOps taught me to automate delivery, improve operational feedback, and build reliable platforms. Teaching helped me explain complex ideas clearly, while operations work taught me to investigate problems methodically.

Moving toward Solutions Architecture is a natural progression from that foundation. It allows me to apply operational experience earlier in the design process, making systems secure, scalable, observable, cost-aware, and practical to run. AWS gives me a platform for turning those architectural decisions into real deployments.

From Certifications to Application

Certifications gave my learning structure and helped me strengthen my foundations in AWS, artificial intelligence, networking, and cloud-native technologies. They also made one limitation clear: passing an exam does not fully demonstrate how someone designs, troubleshoots, or improves a production-style system.

That realization pushed me toward projects I could own end to end. Each project on this site is an opportunity to apply an architectural concept, encounter the friction of implementation, and leave the system better documented and easier to operate than I found it.

Why the Cloud Resume Challenge

I chose the AWS Cloud Resume Challenge because it connects DevOps practice to architecture. It requires more than a static page: the work involves secure hosting, custom domains, HTTPS, automation, serverless services, infrastructure as code, and documentation.

This site now brings together Amazon S3, CloudFront, Route 53, IAM, GitHub Actions, Terraform, Lambda, API Gateway, and DynamoDB. The visitor counter and contact form make the site an active cloud application rather than just a collection of files.

What I Built

  • Static website hosting: Resume and project pages delivered through Amazon S3 and CloudFront.
  • Secure delivery: Custom DNS through Route 53 and HTTPS through CloudFront.
  • Continuous delivery: GitHub workflows that validate and publish website changes.
  • Serverless backend: Lambda, API Gateway, and DynamoDB powering the visitor counter.
  • Infrastructure as code: Terraform configuration for repeatable cloud resource management.
  • Ongoing improvements: A serverless contact form designed with least privilege and practical operational concerns in mind.

Where This Is Going

The Cloud Resume Challenge is the foundation, not the finish line. I plan to continue expanding this portfolio with projects that explore platform engineering, observability, security, generative AI, and resilient system design from an architectural perspective.

For recruiters and hiring managers, this site shows how I am extending my DevOps experience into Solutions Architecture: understand the requirements, make deliberate trade-offs, design for operations, automate repeatable work, and document the result. For fellow engineers, it is an honest record of the decisions, problems, and improvements behind the finished pages.

Explore the AWS Cloud Challenge projects to see the technical implementation, or visit the resume for my experience, skills, and certifications.
